# California Product Closure

A public-facing civic research and policy project examining whether product lifecycle systems remain practically usable at the point where an ordinary resident is holding the actual object.

## Core question

> Does the system remain legible and practically usable at the moment an adult resident is holding the actual object?

The project focuses on the closure of a product lifecycle: repair, reuse, return, recycling, recovery, or lawful disposal.

## Core principle

A product lifecycle should include its closure.

The responsible producer, system, or public authority should provide a reasonably accessible pathway for the material or product to reach an appropriate endpoint.

This does **not** mean every product must be recyclable. It means responsibility, information, funding, access, and physical capacity should be clear enough for the resident to act.

### Practical distinctions

- Identification is not disposition.
- Collection is not closure.
- Movement is not closure.
- A service can exist without being practically accessible.
- A webpage is not a disposal pathway unless sufficient physical, technological, financial, and institutional capacity exists behind it.
- If no verified pathway exists, that absence should be treated as a policy or capacity signal rather than resident failure.

## Resident Outcome Test

After an intervention is implemented:

> Could an ordinary adult resident actually do something differently, more easily, more safely, or more reliably than before?

Outcomes should be described honestly as demonstrated, reported but not independently verified, partial, no demonstrated change, or unknown.

## Research approach

The project combines California policy and regulatory materials, product-stewardship and extended-producer-responsibility research, local government information, and California Civil Grand Jury findings and responses.

A recurring evaluation question is:

> Did this actually change anything?

The goal is not simply to count recommendations or identify services. The goal is to determine whether an intervention changed the resident's practical ability to complete the lifecycle.

## Federal companion question

When material crosses a jurisdictional boundary:

> Does the information and responsibility necessary to manage it remain attached to the material or product record — or does only the material move?
